California is considered one of the most expensive states to live in, and plenty of people have had enough. Since 2020, hundreds of thousands more people have left the state than moved here, [nearly 700,000](https://www.stripes.com/theaters/us/2023-02-15/california-population-decrease-500k-2-years-9148809.html) in just the first two years of the pandemic. A lot of Californians are finally asking themselves if they can still afford to stay. Honestly, I've been asking myself the same thing.

Gas is the first thing everyone brings up, and for good reason. We pay the most in the country, about [$5.70 a gallon](https://www.lendingtree.com/credit-cards/study/us-gas-prices) right now, when the national average is under $4. And it doesn't look like it's getting better anytime soon. Two big refineries closed in the last year, Phillips 66 in Los Angeles and Valero in Benicia, and that's about [17% of the state's refining capacity](https://www.argusmedia.com/de/news-and-insights/latest-market-news/2821046-valero-ends-fuel-production-at-benicia-refinery) gone. Then there's the talk of a mileage tax. It isn't real yet, the state is [still studying it](https://www.govbuddy.com/legislative-tracking/california-ab-1421-road-usage-charge/), but if you drive to work every day, you're paying attention.

Then there's housing. The median home in California costs about [$900,000](https://www.mpamag.com/us/mortgage-industry/market-updates/california-home-sales-fall-amid-mortgage-rate-spike/594114). That's a number a lot of people can't even think about.

When I worked in insurance, I talked to a lot of Californians, and I heard the same thing over and over. Everything keeps going up. Insurance especially. A lot of the big companies have pulled back here, so more people end up on the FAIR Plan, the state's last-resort option, and its rates are going up [an average of 30%](https://www.emarketer.com/content/more-californians-soon-priced-home-insurance) this fall.

The ones that stuck with me were the people on Social Security who can't work anymore. Yes, Social Security goes up a little every year. In January it's going up [3.6%](https://www.ssa.gov/news/en/mip/2026-10-01-f.html), which works out to about $75 a month for the average retiree. But try making $75 matter in California. That's maybe a couple tanks of gas.

When people can't keep up, some of them end up on the street. We have more homeless people than any other state, [181,934](https://www.edhat.com/california/news/new-hud-report-finds-homelessness-declined-in-california-and-nationwide-in-2025/) in the last federal count. It went down a little last year, but you don't have to look hard to see it.

So why do we still love it here?

For me, it's a lot of things. The weather is hard to beat. The people here come from everywhere, and you feel that in the food, the music, the culture. There's so much history. We have some of the best schools and universities in the world. And there's always something going on, whether it's the beach, the mountains, a game or a show.

I was born in Newport Beach and lived there until I was about 7, then my family moved up to the Sacramento area. I live in Cameron Park now. California is the only place I've ever called home.

So where do I land? I want to stay. I really do. But I'm worried about where things are headed.

We've got a new governor coming in January, and that's a chance for things to change, on gas, on housing, on insurance, on just being able to afford to live here. That's a big part of why I started Turnaround California. I want to keep track of what gets promised, what actually happens, and what it means for regular people like us.

If you're asking yourself the same question, I want to hear from you. Staying, leaving, not sure yet, send me your story.
