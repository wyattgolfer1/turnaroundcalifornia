California has always been home for me. But lately, a question I never used to think about keeps coming up, in my own head and in conversations with people all over the state: can we still afford to stay?

I'm not the only one asking. Since 2020, hundreds of thousands more people have moved out of California than have moved in. In just the first two years of the pandemic, the number of people leaving outpaced the number arriving by [nearly 700,000](https://www.stripes.com/theaters/us/2023-02-15/california-population-decrease-500k-2-years-9148809.html).

## The cost of staying

Start at the gas pump. California has the most expensive gas in the country, about [$5.70 a gallon](https://www.lendingtree.com/credit-cards/study/us-gas-prices) on average this month, while the national average has dipped under $4.

And our fuel supply is getting tighter. Two major refineries have shut down in the past year: Phillips 66's Los Angeles refinery at the end of 2025, and Valero's Benicia refinery this spring. Together, they took about [17% of the state's refining capacity](https://www.argusmedia.com/de/news-and-insights/latest-market-news/2821046-valero-ends-fuel-production-at-benicia-refinery) with them. On top of that, lawmakers are [studying a possible per-mile "road charge"](https://www.govbuddy.com/legislative-tracking/california-ab-1421-road-usage-charge/) as a future replacement for the gas tax. Nothing has been decided, but for anyone who drives to work, it's one more thing to worry about.

Then there's housing. The median price of a single-family home in California is now about [$900,000](https://www.mpamag.com/us/mortgage-industry/market-updates/california-home-sales-fall-amid-mortgage-rate-spike/594114). For a lot of people, especially younger Californians, that number makes owning a home feel out of reach.

## What I heard on the job

Before starting Turnaround California, I worked in the insurance industry and talked with Californians from all walks of life. The same story came up again and again: everything just keeps getting more expensive, and insurance is one of the biggest pain points. As major insurers have pulled back, more homeowners have been pushed onto the state's FAIR Plan, the insurer of last resort. Its enrollment has nearly doubled since 2023, and starting this October, its premiums are rising [an average of 30%](https://www.emarketer.com/content/more-californians-soon-priced-home-insurance).

The conversations that stuck with me most were with retirees and people who can no longer work. Social Security does get a yearly raise: benefits will go up [3.6% in January](https://www.ssa.gov/news/en/mip/2026-10-01-f.html), about $75 a month for the average retiree. But when you live on a fixed income in one of the most expensive states in the country, $75 doesn't stretch very far. For many, it barely covers a couple of tanks of gas.

When costs climb faster than paychecks and benefits, some people fall through the cracks entirely. California has more people experiencing homelessness than any other state: [181,934](https://www.edhat.com/california/news/new-hud-report-finds-homelessness-declined-in-california-and-nationwide-in-2025/) in the most recent federal count. That number did drop slightly last year, but it's still a crisis you can see in nearly every city.

## Why we still love it

So why do so many of us stay? Because California is worth loving.

We have some of the best weather on the planet. We live alongside people from every background imaginable, and that diversity shapes our food, our art and our culture. Our history runs deep, and our schools and universities are among the best in the world. And there's always something to do, from beaches and mountains to concerts, sports and entertainment the rest of the country tunes in to watch.

I was born in Newport Beach, grew up in the greater Sacramento area, and now live in Cameron Park. California is the only home I've ever known.

## Where I land

So, stay or go? For me, the answer right now is stay. But I'm staying with real concern for the future.

With a new governor taking office in January, California has a chance to take on the issues pushing people out: gas prices, housing, insurance and the cost of simply getting by. That's what Turnaround California is here to follow. I'll be watching what our leaders promise, what they actually do, and what it means for everyday Californians.

If you're asking yourself the same question, I'd love to hear from you. Whether you're staying, leaving or somewhere in between, send me your story.
